The idea

The rate is universal. The pain is not.

Higher rates are supposed to make everyone spend less. Some borrowers feel it the same month. Others have so much cash, and so much to lose by stopping, that they keep building.

Big Tech can keep building through high interest rates. Most of us can't.

The short version

When inflation runs hot, the Fed raises rates so people and businesses borrow and spend less. That still works. But it does not work evenly. A first-time buyer feels a higher mortgage rate immediately. A company with $100 billion a year in operating cash, racing rivals for AI, can keep pouring concrete.

The economy has to slow by some total amount. If the biggest spenders don't slow down, the rest of us may have to slow down more.

This is a story about uneven effects, not a broken Fed. Housing costs have many causes, and AI is not the main one.

Close to home

Meanwhile, in Lebanon, Indiana

In February 2026, Meta broke ground on a data center campus in Lebanon, the Boone County seat, valued at more than $10 billion. It is planned at about 1 gigawatt of capacity across 4 million square feet, with operations expected at the end of 2027 or early 2028.

Mortgage rates have climbed since then. The building did not stop.

Meta has also pledged to pay the full cost of the site's energy and water service, and to give $1 million a year for 20 years to a local fund that helps residents with energy bills. That raises an interesting question: why would a company help pay its neighbors' electric bills? Data centers increasingly need local consent, and consent has a price.

The other side

Why this isn't the whole story

Big Tech can't ignore rates forever.

Oracle spent $55.7 billion on equipment and buildings last fiscal year while generating $32 billion in operating cash, and borrowed heavily to cover the gap. Amazon's free cash flow fell sharply. The risk didn't disappear. It moved, onto developers, utilities, and private lenders.

Not all of that spending is AI.

Company totals include warehouses, overseas sites, and ordinary equipment replacement.

AI may lower inflation later.

If productivity gains arrive, the economy can produce more without prices rising as fast. Building is inflationary; using may not be.

Keep in mind

What AI did not cause

Housing is expensive for many reasons.

Home prices, too little supply, insurance, taxes, and zoning all matter more directly than data centers.

The projections are projections.

The $9 to $10 trillion buildout estimates assume that announced projects actually get built. Many announced projects never are.

The Fed is not broken.

Rate hikes still work. They just land harder on some borrowers than others, and that was true long before AI.
